What VeloAnalyzer does

A field guide for MTB cross-country and cyclo-cross racing: every tracked start list enriched with official UCI rank and points, and a season of history behind every rider on it. The switcher in the sidebar moves between the two disciplines.

  • 🏁Start lists— UCI rank & points, color-coded by tier
  • 👤Rider profiles— 12 months of history, chart included
  • ⚔Head-to-head— any two riders, every shared race
  • 📊Field strength— nation, team & ranking breakdown
  • 🏆Results— official finishes once a race is run
  • 🔄Always current— rebuilt daily from official UCI data

How points are counted

In MTB cross-country, a rider's UCI points total follows the individual XCO ranking rules (art. 4.16.008): each class of race — World Cup and Championship results are uncapped, Class 1–3 one-day races count the best five, and stage races count the best three — feeds its own quota rather than one flat "best N" across everything. A rider's profile shows exactly which results count toward the total and which were displaced.

Cyclo-cross counts differently (art. C1029): every result counts, in every category but one. Men's juniors are the exception, taking their best six results from Class 1 and 2 races and their best five from junior World Cup rounds.

Ties are broken by the UCI rule: the rider with the more recent point-scoring result ranks higher, regardless of the tier that result came from.

How start lists are ordered

An MTB cross-country start list is ordered by UCI rank, which is what sets the grid. A cyclo-cross one is not: in a year-long series riders line up by their current standing in that series (art. C0919), so the Czech Cup table comes first there and the UCI rank behind it. Once a race has been run, the official result replaces both.

Junior women in cyclo-cross are ranked with the elite women and, at a Class 1 or 2 race, start with them too — one race, one classification. Their pages are the junior riders picked out of that combined field, with the women's UCI ranking behind them, because that is the ranking their grid position actually comes from.

Where the data comes from

Start lists are pulled from organizers' own timing platforms (RaceResult, sportsoft.cz and others), matched to UCI rider records by UCI ID where available and by name and birth year otherwise. Rankings and race history come from the UCI's own data feed and are refreshed daily. Domestic cup standings come from the Czech Cycling Federation's results pages — cpxcmtb.sportsoft.cz for MTB, cpcx.sportsoft.cz for cyclo-cross.

Missing a race?

The home page has a form to suggest a start list that isn't tracked yet — a link to the organizer's or timing provider's entry list is all that's needed.
